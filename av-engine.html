Where the money actually goes

The instinct is that servers are the cost. For this system they are not. OCR is billed per page and the page count is fixed by the business, not by how you deploy — so the same $450 of Mistral OCR and ~$124 of Luna tokens appear in all four options. What changes between options is the wrapper around them.

The 1,000 invoices/day figure drives database rows and matching work, not OCR cost — OCR is charged by page, and ten invoices can share one page-set.

The load is smaller than it sounds. 3,000 pages a day, spread over a day, is about 2 pages a second at peak — and each page is an outbound HTTP call that the worker mostly waits on. The app is I/O-bound and autonomous; only exception cases need a person. Concurrency is not the constraint here, so paying for a big always-on cluster buys nothing.

Option 2 — Container Apps recommended

This is the shape you described: the database is the thing worth protecting, the app layer is disposable. You get a hot standby and automatic failover on Postgres, self-healing containers above it, and no cluster to upgrade, patch or size. Roughly $356/month more than Option 1 and it buys away the single-point-of-failure.

Option 3 — AKS production

The shape the sample BOQ assumes. It is a real production platform and it is the right answer if av-engine is going to sit next to other services on a shared cluster. On its own, for this workload, you are paying about $382/month more than Option 2 for capabilities the app cannot use yet — plus the standing cost of owning cluster upgrades and node image patching.

Option 4 — AKS + HA/DR ceiling

Losing a whole availability zone costs no capacity, and a region failure is a 30–60 minute recovery instead of a day. Worth quoting so the conversation has a top end — still only 63% of the ₹5 lakh budget. Whether the business needs this is a question about the cost of an outage, not about Azure.

Mistral — Document AI is OCR 4 with annotations

Microsoft's own launch post is titled “Mistral Document AI (with OCR 4) and Mistral Medium 3.5 arrive in Microsoft Foundry”. That parenthesis is the whole answer: Document AI is not a separate model from OCR 4 — it is OCR 4 plus annotations. Coverage of the launch states it directly: “Mistral OCR 4 through the API is priced at $4 per 1,000 pages... Document AI is priced at $5 per 1,000 pages.”

So the $5.00 Azure meter named OCR 4 annot glbl Pages is Mistral Document AI. The meters literally named Doc AI * are the older 2505 and 2512 releases — the previous generation, the one OCR 4 supersedes.

Two things this changes for the sheet. First, the +10% data-zone premium: Microsoft applies it to US and EU data-zone deployments. Our models sit in South India on Global deployment, so the premium does not apply — the Azure data-zone meters confirm it (OCR 4 annot DZ is $5.50, exactly 10% over $5.00). Second, batch: the launch coverage cites a 50% Batch-API discount taking OCR 4 to $2/1,000 pages, but that is Mistral's own API. No batch meter for Mistral OCR exists in the Azure rate card — all 18 Mistral meters in South India are on-demand. So I have not modelled a batch saving. Worth asking Microsoft, because at our volume it would be worth ~$225/month.

On the 30 July price cut. You are right that OpenAI cut Luna by 80% — confirmed, to $0.20 in / $1.20 out, effective 30 July 2026. But that is OpenAI's direct API. On Azure it has not landed: every Luna meter still reads $1.00/$6.00 dated 1 July, and the Microsoft moderator on the thread above states plainly that “the OpenAI-announced Luna and Terra pricing changes are not currently reflected in the publicly available Azure OpenAI pricing.” So the BOQ uses $1.00/$6.00 = $124/month. The builder has a “after OpenAI 30-Jul cut” option that drops the same usage to $25/month — use it the day Azure follows.

What did not change

Every infrastructure rate was already read from the same retail API and re-checked in this pass — PostgreSQL Ddsv5 compute ($0.251/$0.502/$1.004 per vCore-hour), storage ($0.131/GB), and Container Apps ($0.000024/vCPU-second, $0.000003/GiB-second) all came back identical. Two rates are derived rather than directly published, and you should treat them as the soft spots in this sheet: Private Endpoint at $0.01/hour + $0.01/GB (not returned for Central India by the API — derived from your reference BOQ, where 6 endpoints + 200 GB = $45.80 reproduces exactly), and ACR Premium at $1.6666/day (the API returns Basic and Standard for Central India but not the Premium registry unit; $50/month matches your reference sheet).

What is worth saying out loud

The budget is not the constraint. The most expensive option here is ₹3.14 lakh — 63% of the ceiling — and the recommended one is ₹1.43 lakh, about 29%. The real question is not what fits in ₹5 lakh; it is how much operational surface you want to own for a system that mostly runs itself.
Pages per PDF is the number to nail down. It is the only assumption that moves the total meaningfully, because OCR is billed per page. Going from 30 to 100 pages per PDF adds $1,050/month on its own — more than the entire compute bill of Option 1. Everything else in this sheet is within noise of it. Move that slider first.
Both AI rates were re-verified against Azure, and one of mine was wrong. I had priced Luna at the Priority Processing meter ($2.00/$12.00).
